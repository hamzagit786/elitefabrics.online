Velvet vs Velour: Pile Weave vs Knitted Stretch

Velvet vs velour: discover the key structural differences between woven luxury velvet and stretchy knit velour. Compare drape, tracksuits, blazers, and washing tips.

Velvet vs Velour: Quick Answer & Main Distinction

In the comparison of velvet vs velour, the essential difference is that velvet is a woven fabric, while velour is a knitted fabric. Velvet is woven on intricate double-cloth looms to create a structured, luxurious, short cut pile ideal for evening gowns, blazers, and upholstery. Velour is a plush loop knit that is cut and brushed, providing 4-way stretch, supreme flexibility, and machine washability for tracksuits and loungewear.

While both boast a soft, fuzzy pile surface that feels wonderful to touch, their structural backbone gives them totally opposite personalities in your wardrobe.

Woven Loom vs. Circular Knit Construction

Examining the back of the fabric reveals their manufacturing origins:

  • Velvet (Woven): Crafted with intersecting warp and weft yarns holding a dense upright pile. The backing is taut and does not stretch unless woven with elastane threads.
  • Velour (Knitted): Produced on circular knitting machines with interlocking yarn loops. The upright pile is created by shearing loop surfaces, giving the fabric natural spring and flexibility.

Stretch, Structure, and Drape Differences

Because velvet is woven, it provides crisp tailoring support. It holds the sharp shoulder silhouette of a tuxedo jacket or the sculptured fullness of heavy living room drapes.

Velour has elastic stretch that contours effortlessly over curves. It moves with active bodies, which is why it became the world’s defining fabric for athletic warmups, dancewear, and vintage 2000s tracksuits.

Formal Eveningwear vs. Casual Loungewear

  • Choose Velvet When: You are attending a black-tie gala, designing an upscale holiday blazer, or reupholstering antique armchairs.
  • Choose Velour When: You want soft plush comfort for traveling, yoga warmups, comfortable baby clothing, or casual weekend hoodies.

Dry Clean Only vs. Machine Washable

Velour is remarkably low maintenance: wash inside out in cold water, and tumble dry on low. Cotton velour will emerge soft and ready to wear without ironing.

Velvet requires strict care: never place an iron face down on velvet pile, and rely on steam or dry cleaning to avoid crushing its upright pile.

Conclusion: The Final Verdict on Velvet vs Velour

Comparing velvet vs velour highlights the contrast between traditional woven luxury and modern knit ease. When you want structured sophistication that turns heads at formal events, velvet is supreme; when you want soft, stretchy comfort for daily relaxation, velour is your go-to choice.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is velour cheaper than velvet?

Yes, circular knitting of velour is faster and less technically demanding than weaving double-cloth velvet, making velour more affordable.

Does velour stretch?

Yes, velour is a knit fabric and has natural multi-directional stretch, often enhanced with elastane or spandex fibers.

Can you iron velour?

Always steam velour or iron on the reverse side on low heat. Direct iron weight on the face can crush the plush pile loops.

Why did velour tracksuits become so popular?

Velour combines the opulent, light-catching look of luxury velvet with the comfortable flexibility and machine washability of everyday sweatpants.
